Lived experience matters

Women's stories belong in climate decisions

Envisioning Resilience connects photography and visual storytelling with climate adaptation. Women document how climate change affects their lives and communities, and share their own perspectives on resilience.

GirlsCARE has worked with the NAP Global Network and Lensational to equip young women in Jamaica with photography and storytelling skills, and create opportunities for their experiences to inform national adaptation conversations.

What do we mean by adaptation?

Adaptation is about responding to the effects of a changing climate. The knowledge and priorities of people living with those effects belong in the decisions about how to respond.

Who it supports

The people behind the climate story

The work in Jamaica has focused on young women and girls from communities experiencing climate vulnerability, including rural and inner-city communities.

Their perspectives are often underrepresented in climate decision-making. Photography provides a way to communicate lived experience, local knowledge, and priorities in their own voices.

We see participants as knowledge holders and agents of change, not simply subjects of a story told by someone else.

How it works

From a camera to a conversation

Creative skills, community experience, and dialogue come together to make women's perspectives visible in adaptation discussions.

  • 01

    Build storytelling skills

    Develop photography and visual storytelling skills, connecting observations of everyday life with wider climate impacts.

  • 02

    Document lived experience

    Create photographs and stories that show how climate change affects lives and communities, and how women envision resilience.

  • 03

    Bring stories into dialogue

    Share the work through exhibitions and policy dialogues, creating space for conversations with adaptation decision-makers.

What changes

Making room for women's knowledge

Participants' photographs and stories have been used in exhibitions and policy dialogues. Their work connects personal and community experience with wider adaptation conversations.

  • Practical creative skills

    Photography and storytelling offer participants tools to document and communicate their own perspectives.

  • Community experience made visible

    Stories bring attention to the ways climate change is experienced in everyday life, through the eyes of women affected by it.

  • A connection to adaptation planning

    Dialogue with decision-makers creates opportunities for lived experience to inform how climate risks and resilience are understood.

  • Pathways for continued advocacy

    Creative practice can be part of a wider journey of learning, advocacy, and professional development beyond the programme.

Stories in action

Beyond the photograph

The story does not end when a photograph is taken. The Envisioning Resilience model creates spaces for women experiencing climate impacts to speak with adaptation decision-makers about what their images reveal.

Jamila Falak's continuing journey

Through Envisioning Resilience, Jamila developed photography and storytelling skills to document climate change and resilience as a young Jamaican woman. Her wider professional and advocacy journey subsequently included a Climate Justice fellowship with Climate Tracker Caribbean and a Chevening scholarship for postgraduate study in the UK.

Working together

Creative practice meets climate justice

The NAP Global Network and Lensational developed the wider Envisioning Resilience initiative. GirlsCARE joined the work in Jamaica, bringing our locally grounded feminist climate justice approach to the collaboration.

Together, the work connects photography and visual storytelling with opportunities for women to contribute their knowledge to adaptation conversations.

NAP Global Network connects the initiative with National Adaptation Plan processes and dialogue with adaptation decision-makers.

Lensational brings its photography and visual storytelling practice, supporting women to develop and share their stories.
