The bigger picture

More than a moment of relief

Lend a Girl a Hand brings GirlsCARE's commitment to care, dignity, and solidarity into practical support for girls, young women, and communities.

Hurricane relief sits within this broader programme. Work such as the Back to School Support Initiative also recognises that support matters in everyday life, not only in the immediate aftermath of a disaster.

Our approach keeps immediate needs in view while recognising the importance of education, wellbeing, and longer-term recovery. Climate resilience is connected to people's ability to care for themselves and one another.

Who it supports

Girls and communities at the centre

The programme centres girls and young women, alongside communities affected by climate-related disasters. It connects practical support with the realities of recovery in Jamaica.

This work extends beyond young people already involved in climate advocacy. Educational needs, wellbeing, and the disruption of everyday life are also part of the climate resilience conversation.

Dignity, not a one-size-fits-all response

We start with people, their priorities, and the relationships of trust built with communities. Girls and young women are knowledge holders and agents of change, not simply recipients of support.

Connected to wider GirlsCARE work

Resilience includes how we live and care

Lend a Girl a Hand is part of GirlsCARE's broader commitment to community resilience. Across the organisation, this includes collective care, wellbeing, and support for climate-resilient livelihoods.

GirlsCARE's wider recovery and resilience work extends to women farmers and other community members whose livelihoods are vulnerable to climate and disaster impacts, particularly in western Jamaica.

Across GirlsCARE's recent activities, work has taken place in St Elizabeth, Trelawny, St James, Hanover, and Westmoreland, including Kilmarnock, Black River, and Sherwood Content.

These organisation-wide connections reflect our understanding that leadership, recovery, livelihoods, and care belong together. They are not separate parts of people's lives.
