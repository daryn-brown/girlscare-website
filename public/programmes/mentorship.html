Young Women’s Climate Justice Mentorship Programme

A Caribbean space for young women to learn from experienced climate and gender justice advocates, build relationships with peers, and strengthen their own leadership.

The purpose

Leadership grows through connection.

GirlsCARE brings climate justice education, feminist analysis, mentoring, and leadership development together. The programme supports young women to understand the issues shaping their communities, develop their own perspectives, and advocate for climate-smart and gender-just solutions.

Participants are treated as emerging leaders and knowledge holders. Learning with experienced advocates and with one another helps turn individual skills into regional relationships and collective power.

Who it supports

Young women shaping climate action.

The programme has supported girls and young women interested in or already engaged in climate action, advocacy, community organising, and social justice. GirlsCARE has intentionally sought to include people from climate-vulnerable communities whose perspectives are often underrepresented in climate decision-making.

Across its cohorts, the programme has connected young women with mentors and peers from different Caribbean communities.

The approach

More than a training session.

Mentorship creates room to learn, practise, and build lasting connections across the Caribbean.

01

Learn with advocates

Young women explore climate and gender justice with experienced advocates and mentors, connecting regional issues to lived realities.

02

Develop a voice

Advocacy, communication, organising, and leadership learning help participants express their own perspectives and engage in decisions affecting their communities.

03

Build collective power

Peer relationships and intergenerational connections create networks of solidarity that can extend beyond a single programme cycle.

Regional connections

A Caribbean mentorship network.

GirlsCARE is based in Jamaica and has connected young women from across the region over multiple programme cycles.

First cohort

Fifteen young women took part, coming from Jamaica, the Cayman Islands, Belize, Antigua and Barbuda, and St Vincent and the Grenadines.

A later programme

A subsequent programme reached 25 young women across the Caribbean.

More recent participation

A more recent programme included young women aged 15–27 from Jamaica, The Bahamas, Trinidad and Tobago, Barbados, and Guyana.

What changes

Knowledge, confidence, and collective voice.

GirlsCARE’s mentorship work is designed to strengthen participants’ understanding of climate and gender justice, build advocacy and organising skills, and support continued climate leadership. Across GirlsCARE’s work, participants have also described growing confidence and peer solidarity, with pathways into further education, advocacy, and professional development.

By 2025, the mentorship programme was in its fifth year. Its Caribbean and intergenerational relationships are part of a larger effort to build collective climate leadership.
