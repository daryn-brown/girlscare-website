About Us

A girl-led Caribbean climate network

GirlsCARE — Girls Climate Action for Resilience & Empowerment — helps women and girls on the frontlines of the climate crisis lead on adaptation, resilience, and their own rights across Jamaica and the Caribbean.

Meet our team

  • Mission

    Equip girls and young women to lead climate action and build resilience in their communities.

  • Vision

    A Caribbean where young women shape a just, climate-resilient future — and share in its power.

  • Approach

    Mentorship, advocacy, research, and community care — led by young women, rooted in local realities.

  • Values

    Feminist, youth-led, community-driven, and grounded in climate justice.

Why GirlsCARE

Why this movement exists

Across the Caribbean, the climate crisis reaches women and girls first and hardest — yet they are too often left out of the decisions that shape their future. GirlsCARE exists to change that.

  • The gap

    Young women live on the frontlines of climate impacts but remain under-represented in climate leadership. We help close that gap.

  • Why girls & young women

    When girls lead, whole communities grow more resilient. Investing in their power is one of the most effective climate strategies there is.

  • Why climate

    Hurricanes, floods, and rising seas threaten Caribbean livelihoods. For us, climate justice and gender justice are inseparable.

The Coordinators

Meet the women leading GirlsCARE

GirlsCARE is guided by young feminist climate leaders working across Jamaica, the Caribbean, and global youth networks.

Ayesha Constable

Ayesha researches and publishes on gender and climate change through her doctoral studies, connecting agriculture, youth, and gender. She is a Global Advisor to FRIDA – the Young Feminist Fund.

Read full bio

She has served on several regional and global youth networks — including the Caribbean Youth Environment Network and Commonwealth Youth Climate Network — is former Caribbean Coordinator for the Sustainable Development Solutions Youth Network, and a regional representative for Global Power Shift (350.org).

Jhannel Tomlinson

Jhannel is a PhD candidate researching community-based adaptation to climate change, and cofounder of GirlsCARE Jamaica and Young People for Action on Climate Change, Jamaica.

Read full bio

She is Caribbean Advisor for the NextGen Climate Board, Sustainability Lead for the JAWiC Board, and the Prime Minister's Youth Awardee for Environmental Protection (2019). She has been named one of "50 Next" youth advancing the future of food and has represented Jamaica as a youth climate champion in Paris, Poland, and Spain.

Programmes

Programmes building climate resilience

From mentorship to disaster response, our initiatives equip girls and young women to act on climate and care for their communities.

01

Flagship Initiative

Mentorship

Girls and young women do not need climate expertise to take part. In plain-language sessions, they learn how climate change affects everyday life, work with mentors and peers, practise speaking and writing, and plan action they can take in their communities.

What participants gain

Practical climate knowledge, confidence to advocate, and a network of people who can support their next step.

See how Mentorship works →
02

Hurricane Relief

When hurricanes strike the Caribbean, GirlsCARE mobilises to support women and girls — coordinating relief, care, and recovery for the communities hit hardest.

Impact

Connects women and girls with dignity supplies, community care, and recovery support after severe weather.

See the impact →
03

Envisioning Resilience

A creative space where girls imagine and design climate-resilient futures for their communities through storytelling, research, and reflection.

Impact

Creates space for girls to shape and share locally grounded visions for climate-resilient communities.

See the impact →
04

Care Collective

Our peer network connecting young women across the region for solidarity, knowledge-sharing, and collective climate advocacy.

Impact

Strengthens regional solidarity, knowledge-sharing, and collective climate advocacy among young women.
