What changes

Skills, confidence, and collective power

Across GirlsCARE's work, participants develop knowledge, relationships, and opportunities to lead. These themes describe the breadth of the work, not identical outcomes for every participant.

  • 01

    Climate and gender justice knowledge

    Strengthening understanding of climate change, climate justice, and gender justice.

  • 02

    Skills to organise and advocate

    Developing advocacy, communication, organising, and leadership skills.

  • 03

    Connections across the Caribbean

    Connecting with mentors and peers whose experience and perspectives support continued learning.

  • 04

    Solidarity and collective support

    Building networks where people can learn from one another, share resources, and act together.

  • 05

    Independent perspectives

    Developing and communicating participants' own perspectives on climate change.

  • 06

    Confidence to participate

    Taking part more confidently in climate advocacy and decision-making spaces.

  • 07

    Photography and storytelling

    Developing practical creative skills through work such as Envisioning Resilience.

  • 08

    Lived experience in policy conversations

    Connecting community experience with wider climate policy and adaptation discussions.

  • 09

    Pathways to keep growing

    Identifying and pursuing further education, advocacy, and professional-development opportunities.

Milestones

How the work has grown

  1. 01

    A sustained Caribbean mentorship platform

    By 2025, the Young Women's Climate Justice Mentorship Programme was in its fifth year. Across multiple cohorts, it has connected young women with climate justice leaders, feminist activists, and peers across the Caribbean.

    Explore Mentorship →
  2. 02

    Women's stories entering adaptation conversations

    Through Envisioning Resilience, GirlsCARE worked with the NAP Global Network and Lensational to develop photography and storytelling skills. Participants' photographs and stories have been used in exhibitions and policy dialogues with decision-makers.

    Explore Envisioning Resilience →
  3. 03

    A broader understanding of resilience and care

    The work has expanded beyond climate advocacy training into community care, psychosocial wellbeing, livelihood resilience, disaster recovery, and practical support for girls and women affected by climate impacts.

    Explore our wider priorities →

Climate leadership is connected to people's ability to recover, care for themselves and others, maintain livelihoods, and take part in decisions affecting their lives.

A participant's continuing journey

Jamila Falak: beyond the programme

Envisioning Resilience · Jamaica

Photography and storytelling became part of Jamila's wider advocacy journey, which later included a Climate Justice fellowship with Climate Tracker Caribbean and a Chevening scholarship.

Her story offers one perspective on the pathways that can continue beyond participation in a programme.

Programme Impact

What change looks like in practice

Each initiative meets a different need while building the same foundation: informed, connected, and resilient young women.

Flagship programme

Mentorship

Who it supports: Girls and young women interested in or already engaged in climate action, advocacy, community organising, and social justice, including those whose perspectives are underrepresented in climate decision-making.

The programme strengthens climate justice knowledge, advocacy skills, and relationships that support young women's leadership.

Practical support and recovery

Lend a Girl a Hand

Hurricane relief is part of Lend a Girl a Hand, alongside work such as the Back to School Support Initiative. The broader programme connects practical support with care and the longer-term needs of girls and communities.

Programme focus

  • Hurricane relief and community support
  • Back to School and educational support
  • Dignity, wellbeing, and longer-term recovery
Explore Lend a Girl a Hand →

Visual storytelling in Jamaica

Envisioning Resilience

Working with the NAP Global Network and Lensational, GirlsCARE supports young women in Jamaica to develop photography and storytelling skills and bring lived experience into adaptation conversations.

Impact focus

  • Practical photography and visual storytelling skills
  • Women's own accounts of climate impacts and resilience
  • Exhibitions and dialogue with adaptation decision-makers
Explore Envisioning Resilience →

Collective care and wellbeing

Care Collective

Care Collective brings connection, reflection, and wellbeing alongside activism and leadership. Shared sessions and retreat spaces reflect GirlsCARE's commitment to caring for the people behind the work.

Programme focus

  • Relationships of trust, connection, and solidarity
  • Shared knowledge and collective learning
  • Care as an ongoing part of sustainable leadership

Mentorship across the Caribbean

First cohort

The first cohort included young women from Jamaica, Cayman Islands, Belize, Antigua and Barbuda, and St Vincent and the Grenadines.

More recent participation

A more recent programme included young women aged 15–27 from Jamaica, The Bahamas, Trinidad and Tobago, Barbados, and Guyana.

These are historical cohort examples, not current eligibility criteria or an all-programme country total.

Community work in Jamaica

GirlsCARE's newer recovery and resilience work extends to women farmers, girls, young women, and communities affected by climate-related disasters, particularly in western Jamaica.
